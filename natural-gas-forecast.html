Natural Gas Price Forecast

Modeling trend and seasonality in natural gas prices to produce a 12-month forward forecast.

Overview

Given monthly historical natural gas price data, the goal is to estimate the purchase price at any date — past or future. Prices between two points in the historical record are estimated by linear interpolation. Prices for the year ahead are extrapolated from the trend and seasonality of the historical data.

Method

The trend is estimated with a 12-month moving average, which turns out to be approximately linear. Trend and seasonality are then modeled jointly with a linear regression combined with a Fourier series. The order of the Fourier series is chosen by trading off the model's RMSE against the p-value of a Shapiro-Wilk test on the residuals — as the order increases, RMSE keeps falling but the residuals drift further from normal, so an order of 6 was chosen as the balance point. Built in Python with pandas, matplotlib, and scipy.stats.

Result

Historical and forecast values are combined into a single series and interpolated to daily frequency. Past-date estimates simply read off this interpolated series. Future estimates come from the fitted model, with a 3-sigma confidence interval derived from the historical residuals, under the simplifying assumption that their variance stays constant over time.
