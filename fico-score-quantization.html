FICO Score Quantization

Finding the optimal binning of FICO credit scores with a genetic algorithm.

Overview

A FICO score is a standardized credit score, produced by the Fair Isaac Corporation, that rates a borrower's creditworthiness on a scale from 300 to 850. It's used in around 90% of US mortgage lending decisions. The goal here is to predict a borrower's probability of default from their FICO score, while quantizing the continuous score into a small number of bins that stay easy to interpret and act on.

Method

The bin boundaries are found with a genetic algorithm, where each gene is the value of one threshold separating two bins. The search is framed as a multi-objective optimization: the fitness function rewards the log-likelihood of the resulting partition against the observed default data, and penalizes any partition where default rates don't move monotonically across bins. Implemented in Python with pandas, numpy, and the pygad genetic algorithm library.

Result

The optimal quantization found by the algorithm produces default rates that decrease monotonically as FICO score increases across every bin — a desirable property for a creditworthiness model, since it means the bins stay consistent with borrowers' actual credit risk.
